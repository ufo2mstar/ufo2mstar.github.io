# PreScript:
I have been chewing on this one for years now, usually mid-meal, usually losing the argument inside my own head. :sweat_smile:

Someone across the table is gleefully tipping back a davara tumbler of filter coffee hot enough to sterilize surgical instruments, or chasing a curry that has clearly stopped being food and become a `dare`. And someone else (often me) is wincing, blowing on the spoon, doing the obligatory ninety-second wait. And the unspoken scoreboard in the room always reads the same way:

> the one who feels less is somehow the tougher, more authentic, more disciplined human being.

**I have also been the smug one.** I have absolutely sat there thinking _this person has wrecked their palate; mine is the one that still works._ It cuts both ways.

Because this little dinner-table standoff turns out to be a remarkably clean specimen of one of philosophy's oldest puzzles: the `problem of other minds`. And the spoiler is that *both* of us are quietly getting it wrong. :fire:

# Hot!
Two very different things hide under the one word "hot": **capsaicin heat** (chillies) and **thermal heat** (temperature). Both trip the *same* receptor, [TRPV1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRPV1), which is why the brain files them under the same `burning` (and why so many languages, though not all, use one word for both). But the resemblance is a con:

> Capsaicin doesn't damage anything. It just *convincingly lies* to your nervous system.

A chilli pulls the fire alarm without starting a fire. The pain is real; the injury is not. So building capsaicin tolerance (via receptor `downregulation`) is largely harmless, and frankly kind of joyful. Paul Rozin had a wonderful name for the pleasure of chasing a stimulus you know is safe:

> **benign masochism** - the roller-coaster of the tongue.

Thermal heat is a whole other animal. Above ~60&deg;C it is not bluffing. It is doing **actual tissue damage**. Which means thermal "tolerance" is not the same trick as chilli tolerance at all:

> with the chilli, the pain is a lie. With the kettle, the pain was telling the truth... and the people who seem most unbothered may have simply lost the ability to hear the warning.

That kind of tolerance is, in real part, **dulled and dead nerve endings** plus mucosal scarring, the very sensors that were supposed to flag danger. And it isn't a free quirk: the WHO's [IARC](https://www.iarc.who.int/) files drinks above **65&deg;C** as a **Group 2A carcinogen** ("probably carcinogenic"), and the cultures that prize near-boiling tea and mate show the esophageal-cancer numbers to match.

One fact reframes the whole standoff... and neither side at the table usually has it:

# Other minds
To step back, and address the actual subject here:
The classic [`problem of other minds`](https://iep.utm.edu/other-minds/) is the quiet worry that I can never *directly* get at your experience. I only ever infer it from the outside, and I paper over the gap with imagination: I stub my toe, you wince in sympathy, we assume our inner movies roughly match.

But our heat-habituated friend is stuck with a harder version, because there is an extra layer of **sensory distortion** in the way:

> when someone says "that's too hot", they run it through their own recalibrated model and it simply *does not compute*. To them the temperature is fine. Unremarkably fine, even.

It is like asking someone deaf since childhood to *feel* their way into "loud". They can know it intellectually; they cannot summon it. The instrument they'd use to imagine your experience has itself been retuned.

## The asymmetry (and why it's nobody's fault)
This is the bit that generalizes far past the dinner table:

> the one with intact senses can, with some effort, imagine their way into the habituated one's world. The reverse is genuinely uphill.

If I find the coffee scalding, I can reason myself over to their side easily: _they grew up on this, their nerves adapted, this honestly feels normal to them._ One small model of adaptation and I'm there. But for them to reach *me*, they have to argue against their own body, which keeps overruling them with _no, that's fine_.

So grace flows easily one direction and uphill the other. And the lesson I keep wanting to skip is that this should make me **more patient with them, not more pleased with myself.** Harder-for-them is not the same as wrong-of-them.

# The wincer is no saint either
The sensitive party (me) has a favorite line: _at least my nerves still work._ Tidy. Flattering. And also its own failure to imagine the other complexly.

Because wrapped inside that scalding tumbler is not *just* nerve damage. There's a grandmother's kitchen in there, a cultural rite, the genuine `benign masochism` thrill, an identity, decades of comfort and belonging. To flatten all of that down to "accumulated tissue loss" is to do to them *exactly* what they do to me: reduce a whole person to a single deficient axis.

And notice both of us reach for the same broken move, the `appeal to nature` [fallacy I've ranted about before](/blog/2017/10/30/misplaced_metaphors/):

> "more heat = tougher = better" is a naturalistic fallacy... and "more sensitive = finer = superior" is the very same fallacy, just wearing the other team's jersey.

# The pathetic part: nobody wins
Two people, one bowl of soup, two completely private universes. The pepper hasn't changed; the soup hasn't changed. The only thing manufactured at that table is the contempt, and it gets manufactured on *both* sides.

Years ago I wrote that once you bother to [imagine someone more complexly](/blog/2017/11/27/mind_inputs_and_opinions/), their position stops looking absurd. Same thing here, in both directions. When you *don't* imagine the other complexly, you quietly assume their inner experience is just a worse, weaker, cruder version of your own. The habituated one files me under `delicate`. I file them under `damaged`. Both of us are wrong in the identical way.

And the real loss isn't the temperature of anyone's coffee. It's that two people who could have been genuinely *curious* about a fascinating difference in wiring instead just filed each other away and ate in silent judgment. Nobody walks away richer. That's the pathetic bit.

# Bottomline
There is no winner here to crown, and I think wanting one is the mistake.

The honest facts are worth knowing, on both sides of the table, not as ammunition but as humility: capsaicin lies, heat doesn't, and whatever feels like your `normal` was mostly *built*, not chosen. I'll still heed my own body's warning (I'm not about to start mainlining boiling rasam to prove a point :sweat_smile:) but I'd like to hold that without the sneer.

> perception is not reality. Two people eat the same pepper; one suffers, one delights. The pepper never changed.

So, the whole ballgame: like the Green brothers like to say, **imagine the other one complexly.** I keep needing to relearn to apply it. :coffee:
